HVS PET CARE INFORMATION

Helpful pet-care guides

This page provides general educational information for pet owners. It is not a diagnosis and does not replace advice from a qualified veterinary professional.

Preparing for a home pet visit

Before a home appointment, write down what you have noticed about your pet, when the problem began, changes in eating or drinking, recent medicines or treatments, vaccination information and any previous veterinary history you have available.

Keep the pet in a calm, safe area and make sure the professional can access the location. A clear address, landmark or GPS location can reduce delays.

Pet vaccination basics

Vaccination helps protect pets against specific infectious diseases. The right schedule depends on the animal, age, health status, previous vaccination history and local veterinary recommendations.

Do not choose or change a vaccination schedule based only on online information. Ask a qualified veterinary professional what is appropriate for your pet.

When a pet may need urgent help

Some changes should not wait for a routine appointment. Examples include severe breathing difficulty, collapse, uncontrolled bleeding, major injury, repeated seizures, severe weakness or other signs that the animal is in immediate danger.

Important: If you believe your pet is seriously ill or injured, seek prompt veterinary care. A home-visit service may not be appropriate for an emergency.

Keeping basic pet information ready

It can be useful to keep vaccination records, previous prescriptions, the name and contact details of your regular veterinary clinic, and a current list of medicines or supplements your pet receives. This information can help a veterinary professional understand the situation more quickly.
